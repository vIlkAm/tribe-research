01 / PRODUCT PRINCIPLE

What the interface is actually showing

The product should look like an advanced “brain + behavior” analysis system, but every visual must make clear which layer is measured, predicted, or merely a research proxy.

Observed

Real platform outcomes. Views, watch time, retention, shares, saves, comments, likes, completion, velocity and account context.

ground truth

Predicted behavior

Model outputs. Expected 3s/5s hold, completion, share rate, save rate, replay likelihood and normalized distribution potential.

trained prediction

Neural proxies

Research-derived latent signals. Attention, value, social relevance, cognitive friction, sensory drive and memorability proxies.

not literal intent
v0.2 data In the v0.2 data only the neural-proxy layer exists. predictions.status is not_available until a behavior model is trained on real outcomes; observed metrics arrive later from an exported outcome file.
Never write "brain firing" in the product. TRIBE v2 predicts cortical fMRI BOLD responses on an average-subject cortical surface. BOLD is a slow hemodynamic signal related to neural activity, not a direct recording of spikes. Use copy such as predicted cortical response, neural proxy, or brain-response estimate. [S1] When a proxy rises strongly, say "strong predicted response" (e.g. "Strong predicted attention capture"), not "firing".

North-star interaction

The editor scrubs the video and sees what changes in the predicted audience response at that exact moment, then receives a concrete reason and an edit hypothesis. The timeline is the primary product; the 3D brain is the visual explainer.

03 / SIGNAL TAXONOMY

What should be displayed as a "good" neural response?

Do not encode one universal direction. Some useful content correlates involve increased activity; preferred short-video viewing has also been associated with deactivation of dACC/dlPFC relative to videos that viewers terminate early. The frontend therefore displays pattern-specific proxies, not “more = better”. [S3]

04 / TIMELINE (CONT.)

Timing rules and moments

Timing rules

  • Grid. Values sit on a 2 Hz display grid from t = 0 (timing.display_hz); values[k] covers [k/2, (k+1)/2) s.
  • Steps. The grid is filled by sample-and-hold from the native TRIBE step (timing.native_tr_s, 1.0 s in the sample). Draw steps; do not smooth or imply finer resolution.
  • Gaps. null = no prediction (TRIBE drops segments with no events; spans in timing.gaps_ms). Leave a visible gap/hatch; never interpolate.
  • Onset. timing.onset_window_ms [0, 2000] is the onset response. Shade it lightly and exclude it from “strongest moment” call-outs (moment.in_onset_window). The z scale is estimated outside it (within_clip_z_excluding_onset).
  • Time base. time_base: "stimulus": the 5 s hemodynamic lag is already removed, so t lines up with the video frame.

Moment rules (moments.py)

  • kind: attention_drop, broad_response (≥ 3 channels rising together), proxy_rise, proxy_fall.
  • Runs enter at |z| ≥ 1.0, stay while |z| ≥ 0.5, last ≥ 1.5 s; a gap ends a run. At most 12 moments.
  • attention_drop: a fall run ≥ 1.5 z below the highest attention level in the preceding 3 s. Evidence adds static_visual (no shot boundary from 1 s before the run to its end), speech_continues (speech covers ≥ half the span), language_load_high (mean language z > 0.5).
  • Only drops carry a hypothesis + test_metric (status edit_hypothesis_untested); everything else is an observation. severity ranks within this clip only.

v0.2 data Behavior deltas require the behavior model (P1). Until then a comparison can only place the neural lanes of two analyses side by side, and each stays relative to its own clip: within-clip z-values are not comparable across clips, so no neural delta should be computed either.

Diff UX

  • Align variants by semantic moment as well as timestamp when cuts change duration.
  • Show deltas in predicted behavior first; neural differences second, as which moments and channels differ between cuts, never as z-value deltas (each clip is normalised on its own).
  • Each delta must show confidence and model version.
  • Allow “publish as experiment” to create a backend experiment object and later attach real outcomes.
Long-term loop: recommendation → edited variant → real post → measured uplift → training example. That feedback loop is the defensible asset; the brain visualization is a feature layer, not the moat.

Region hover (assets.region_map)

  1. Map the cursor position inside the current brain tile to idmap pixel coordinates (scale by tile_w/tile_h).
  2. Sample the lossless idmap PNG at native size with smoothing off (never the JPEG sprite); format as lowercase #rrggbb.
  3. Look it up in ids → channel key. #000000 = no channel: no tooltip.
  4. Tooltip: label, basis.regions_text, current z (values[floor(t × display_hz)]) and copy.tooltip.

The idmap is static per ROI-map version. legend_src shows channel colours, directions and the colormap.

Gaps and onset

If t falls in timing.gaps_ms (no tile covers it), dim the brain panel and label it “no prediction”. Never hold the previous tile silently. Tiles inside the onset window get the same light “onset response” note as the timeline.

assets.research_vertex_map

Same sprite shape with mode: "research_vertex" (brain_vertex.jpg in real bundles): the raw per-vertex prediction. Show it only when Research mode is enabled. Absent from the synthetic sample. Optional summary_png and demo_mp4 are also absent there.

Colour is relative. Red/blue shows a region above/below its own average in this clip. It does not mean good/bad and is not comparable across clips; regions below the 0.5 z threshold are faded.

11 / RESEARCH BASIS

Research references used for the UI taxonomy

  1. [S1] TRIBE v2. d’Ascoli et al. (2026), A foundation model of vision, audition, and language for in-silico neuroscience. Official repository describes multimodal prediction onto the fsaverage5 cortical surface (~20k vertices). github.com/facebookresearch/tribev2
  2. [S2] BOLD Moments Dataset. 1,102 three-second videos, fMRI responses from 10 subjects, rich labels including memorability. Nature Communications (2024); dataset repo.
  3. [S3] Preferred short-video viewing. Hong et al. (2026), Brain activity inhibition during Short Video Viewing. Preferred videos watched to completion showed greater dACC/dlPFC deactivation than less-preferred videos terminated early. doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2026.121722
  4. [S4] Sharing and social relevance. Scholz et al., The Value of Sharing Information. Value, self-related and social-cognition systems were associated with selection/sharing; dmPFC/TPJ showed sharing-specific associations in whole-brain analyses. PMC5507728
  5. [S5] Neural synchrony and audience preference. Dmochowski et al. (2014), Audience preferences are predicted by temporal reliability of neural processing. Nature Communications 5:4567
  6. [S6] EEG Moments Dataset. 1,102 three-second videos, 128-channel EEG and eye tracking from 6 subjects; same stimuli as BOLD Moments. arXiv:2608.28768; OpenNeuro mirror.
  7. [S7] SmartEdit. Gupta et al. (ICME 2025), edit-signal-based short-video engagement prediction and improvement suggestions. Adobe Research.
  8. [S8] SnapUGC / VQualA. Large real-world short-video engagement dataset and ICCV 2025 challenge; engagement models benefit from multimodal video/audio/text features. ICCV Open Access.
  9. [S9] HCP-MMP1 atlas. Glasser et al. (2016), A multi-modal parcellation of human cerebral cortex. Nature 536:171–178. fsaverage projection from figshare 3498446, mirrored at github.com/tannerjared/HCP-MMP1.

Scientific interpretations here are design priors, not validated biomarkers of virality. Product claims should be calibrated only after held-out evaluation on the company’s own platform outcomes.
